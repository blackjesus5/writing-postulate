# Approaches this skill keeps

Writing Postulate does not install other skills. It keeps a few working methods and refuses the parts that would flatten every page into one voice, or that would change the meaning to sound more human.

There are two layers.

**Registers** are the approaches you choose for a reader. A summary, a README, a procedure, a lesson, an essay, a compassionate explanation, a letter, an evidence brief, and a marketing note are different jobs. The choice guide is [GUIDE.md](../GUIDE.md). The same facts written each way are in [examples/by-purpose.md](../examples/by-purpose.md).

**Methods** are the approaches underneath those registers. They say what good prose owes the reader no matter which register you picked. This page explains those methods.

## Beautiful Prose

Use this when the page is an essay, a letter, or any explanation that a person will read for more than a scan.

Keep concrete nouns, openings that already contain the subject, and a cadence that changes with the weight of the sentence. A longer sentence can carry a trade-off. A short sentence can let a fact settle. Drop filler and decorative groups of three.

Refuse a cold voice as the default. Refuse a ban on headings. A README and a procedure need headings. Refuse imitation of a named writer. The aim is precise English for this material, not a costume.

## Simple English

Use this when the reader must understand a mechanism or follow a step.

Keep the mechanism in the first sentences. Define an unfamiliar term once. In a procedure, write the condition before the command, and give one action per step. State the limit in the same plain language as the rule.

Refuse a global word cap. Refuse a maintenance-manual voice for a letter or an essay. Short words do not replace a term the field already uses. Gloss the term. Do not swap it for a vague English substitute.

## Humanizer, and a review for chatbot prose

Use this as a last pass on a draft, not as a new personality.

Look for filler, vague claims, inflated significance, promotional language, forced contrasts, and a rhythm that repeats the same sentence shape. Replace “not X, but Y” with the fact. Cut closers that repeat the opening.

Refuse the idea that “human” means evading an AI detector. Refuse a rewrite that changes a number, a command, a citation, or the source’s uncertainty in order to sound looser. A score is not a measure of whether the page is true.

## Summarize

Use this when the reader has a decision, a hand-off, or a question, and the source is longer than the page they can use.

Keep the depth matched to that need. A quick orientation keeps the main claim. A decision brief keeps evidence, uncertainty, options, and the next action. A research brief keeps disagreements and limitations. Say what the source says before you say what it means.

Refuse compression that turns a possibility into a fact, or that drops the doubt because the paragraph got shorter.

## Documentation

Use this before drafting a README, a spec, or a procedure that depends on a product’s behavior.

Name the product and version, the reader’s question, the authoritative source, and one example that proves the explanation. Cite the source when the answer depends on it. For a procedure, put the risk with the action.

Refuse product folklore that the documentation does not support. If the source does not say it, the page does not say it.

## Document formats

Markdown, DOCX, PDF, and PPTX are not voices. They are structure.

Keep headings, tables, lists, notes, citations, named styles, page or slide order, and tracked changes unless the person asks to change the structure. Extract that structure before rewriting the sentences. Inspect the finished file in its own format.

The format does not decide whether the prose is a summary, a lesson, or a letter. The register does.

## Marketing and developer relations

Use this only when the task is positioning, a launch, a landing page, search, conversion, distribution, or measurement.

The sequence is fixed: language the customer already used, then what the product actually does, then proof, then one action, then the channel, then the metric. A release post, a thread, and a slide can differ in length. They carry the same claim.

Refuse claims the shipped artifact cannot support. Refuse quotations you invented. Refuse urgency, scarcity, consensus, fear, and social proof that the source does not contain. Refuse a thin page padded with search terms. Refuse a funnel frame on a technical explanation, a compassionate page, or a private document.

## Craft from a private conversation

A private two-speaker analysis contributed general habits, not a voice to copy.

Orient the reader before the deep architecture. Name the surface label, then the underlying mechanism. Put a concrete instance before the abstraction. Pace a claim. Repair a jump by restating it at a deeper level. Leave room for what the other person knows. After a layered paragraph, let a short sentence settle the claim. Warmth stays steady and non-evaluative. A personal story is evidence the writer can own. It is not a diagnosis.

The recording, the transcript, the speaker names, and the private analysis stay out of this repository. The skill does not imitate either speaker.

## Summary of decisions

| Approach | Kept | Refused |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Beautiful Prose | Concrete nouns, varied cadence, substantive openings, no filler or decorative triplets | Coldness as a default, a universal ban on headings, imitation of a named register |
| Simple English | Mechanism first, defined terms, condition before command, one action per procedure step | Global word caps and a maintenance-manual voice for letters or essays |
| Humanizer | Detection of filler, vague claims, inflated significance, repetitive syntax | “Human” as detector evasion, rewriting that changes meaning |
| Humanize AI Text | A review pass for chatbot artifacts, promotional language, and forced contrasts | AI probability scores and bypass claims |
| Summarize | Reader-purpose and adjustable-depth summaries | Compression that removes uncertainty or decision context |
| Documentation | Question, authoritative source, version, and example before drafting | Claims the documentation does not support |
| Office formats | Structure preserved, rendered file checked | Treating the file format as a prose voice |
| Marketing and DevRel | Customer language, product truth, proof, one action, then the channel | Invented quotes, manufactured urgency, keyword padding, funnel frames on other registers |
| Private conversation | Orientation, instance before abstraction, room for the other person, a short settling sentence | Publishing the recording or imitating either speaker |
