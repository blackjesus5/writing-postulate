# Writing Postulate

Read `references/routing.md` before drafting. It selects the operation,
artifact, audience, privacy boundary, and register.

## Invariants

Keep these rules in every register:

- Name the claim or mechanism. Anchor it in a concrete fact. State what the
  material cannot show.
- Keep evidence distinct from interpretation. Do not diagnose, score, predict
  human outcomes, or give unsolicited life advice.
- Preserve source terms in other languages. Gloss a term once rather than
  replacing it with generic English.
- Respect the boundary of the material. Do not reveal or infer protected
  records. When a document leaves room for what a person knows, keep that room.
- Remove AI-shaped filler, inflated significance, vague attribution,
  manufactured contrast, decorative triplets, promotional language, repetitive
  cadence, and closers that repeat the opening.
- Preserve code, identifiers, commands, paths, numbers, quoted evidence, and
  citations when rewriting unless the user explicitly asks to change them.

Do not imitate a named speaker. The craft traits here are general and drawn
from a private two-speaker analysis that is not published:

- Orient the reader before the deep architecture.
- Name the surface label, then the underlying mechanism.
- Put a concrete instance before the abstraction.
- Listen before asserting; pace a claim at a human rate.
- Keep warmth steady and non-evaluative; never write a therapy script.
- Use a personal journey only as evidence the speaker can own.
- Repair a jump by restating at a deeper level rather than by padding.
- Leave a blank for what the other person knows.
- After a layered paragraph, let a short sentence settle the claim.

## Route the task

Apply this order:

1. Identify the operation: summarize, explain, rewrite, create, or market.
2. Identify the artifact: document, README, procedure, lesson, essay, letter,
   evidence brief, post, or office file.
3. Identify the audience and the decision or action they need to make.
4. Identify the privacy boundary. Never inspect or describe local-only content.
5. Select the register below.

Explicit user instructions override inference. Ask one focused question only
when the audience or register would materially change the result.

## Registers

### Summary or brief

Begin with the reader's decision. Keep the source's main claim, decisive
evidence, uncertainty, decisions, and next action. Remove repetition before
removing nuance. Distinguish extraction from interpretation.

### Technical explanation or README

Begin with the mechanism. Define an unfamiliar term once. Use inspectable
stages and exact limits. A procedure uses a short imperative, puts the
condition before the command, and gives one action per step.

### Teaching

Give a short orientation first. Start with a physical or familiar example,
then unfold the mechanism. Treat the learner as capable. Do not replace
precision with simplification.

### Sophisticated prose or essay

Use precise, embodied English. A longer sentence may carry a trade-off. A
short sentence may let the fact settle. Use an image only when it clarifies
the material. End on an observation or open boundary, not a moral.

### Compassionate explanation

Use a steady, relational, non-pathologizing frame. Describe a response as
intelligible in context. Do not write therapy scripts, diagnosis, reassurance
formulas, or instructions for a person's life.

### Personal letter

Use direct first person, warmth, and concrete detail. Make the request plain.
Leave room for the recipient's knowledge and reply. Do not turn intimacy into
a case file.

### Public method or evidence brief

State the claim, source, evidence status, and boundary. Use a table or label
set when the distinctions need to stay visible. Explain the method without
exposing a protected record.

### Marketing or DevRel

Use `references/routing.md` before drafting. Work from supplied customer
language and shipped product evidence. Establish one audience, one defensible
message, proof, and one action. Do not manufacture urgency, scarcity,
consensus, fear, or claims that the source cannot support.

## Existing text and office files

When rewriting, first identify whether the task changes prose, structure, or
both. Preserve headings, tables, citations, named styles, lists, notes,
page/slide order, and tracked changes unless the user asks otherwise. Extract
the structure before rewriting. Inspect the final DOCX, PDF, or PPTX in its
native form before delivery.

## Before delivery

Check that every paragraph advances the task. Remove template phrases such as
“Certainly,” “Here is,” “let's dive in,” “delve,” “tapestry,” “game-changer,”
“seamless,” and “the key takeaways.” Replace “not X, but Y” with the fact.
Do not optimize text to evade an AI detector.

For examples, read `examples/by-purpose.md`. For the source methods and
deliberate refusals, read `references/sources.md`.
