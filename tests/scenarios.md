# Scenarios

These scenarios check routing and boundaries. They are not model benchmarks.

## Summary

Prompt: “Summarize this incident report for the person who must decide whether to pause the rollout.”

Pass:

- States the decision, evidence, uncertainty, and next action.
- Removes repetition without turning a possibility into a fact.
- Does not add a motivational conclusion.

## Technical explanation

Prompt: “Explain why the export job can hear a pitch but cannot name the string.”

Pass:

- Starts with the mechanism.
- Defines an unfamiliar term once.
- States the limit in plain language.

## Procedure

Prompt: “Write a recovery step when the parser cannot fetch a page.”

Pass:

- Puts the condition before the command.
- Gives one action per step.
- Keeps commands, paths, and quoted errors unchanged.

## Teaching

Prompt: “Explain a segment graph to a student who has never worked with city data.”

Pass:

- Gives a short orientation.
- Uses one concrete example before the abstraction.
- Does not talk down to the reader.

## Literary prose

Prompt: “Write a short public essay about a route planner that keeps uncertainty visible.”

Pass:

- Uses cadence and an image only where they clarify.
- Does not invent facts, metaphorical stakes, or a grand conclusion.

## Compassionate explanation

Prompt: “Explain why a person may need a short entry path before a long record.”

Pass:

- Is steady and relational.
- Does not diagnose, give therapy language, or offer unsolicited advice.

## Personal letter

Prompt: “Write a short note asking a collaborator to review the winter trail limits.”

Pass:

- Uses direct first person and a plain request.
- Leaves room for the recipient's reply and knowledge.
- Does not turn the relationship into a case file.

## Evidence brief

Prompt: “State what the planner can claim from its 1,240 verified records.”

Pass:

- Separates claim, source, evidence status, and boundary.
- Does not invent snow, closures, or fitness conclusions.
- Keeps protected or local-only material undescribed.

## Marketing

Prompt: “Draft a launch post for a feature that lets a user compare two routes.”

Pass:

- Uses a supplied customer phrase or asks for one.
- Names a real capability, proof, and one action.
- Does not manufacture scarcity, consensus, or urgency.

## Format preservation

Prompt: “Rewrite this DOCX policy for a new audience.”

Pass:

- Extracts structure before changing prose.
- Preserves named styles, headings, tables, citations, and tracked changes.
- Checks the rendered output before delivery.
